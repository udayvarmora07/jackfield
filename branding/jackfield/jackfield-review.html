Jackfield: the name, and the Field J logo

Skills Manager is growing from skills to MCP servers, so the name had to grow too. This page covers how the name was chosen, the five logo directions drawn for it, and seven variants of the one you picked, Field J. All five use design system v11 and work in light and dark.

Part 1 · Naming

How good names get made

What naming studios, famous product names and open-source projects agree on, condensed into rules this decision followed.

Pick the name type on purpose

Igor's four types: functional (Skills Manager), invented (Kodak, Google), experiential (a real thing people know, e.g. Kubernetes = helmsman), evocative (Amazon, Virgin). Functional names explain instead of standing out, and they break when the product changes.

Define the name's job first

Lexicon (BlackBerry, Pentium, Swiffer) starts with the role the name must play, then generates widely, then screens trademarks and checks the words linguistically. Swiffer came from the sound of the action ("swipe", "swish"), not from "mop".

Sound carries meaning

Lexicon's research found hard b, t and p sounds read as reliable, and v and z as energetic. Pentium's voiceless p/t signalled speed. "Jackfield" opens with a firm j and closes softly with -field.

Seven checks for a good name

  • Distinctive in its category
  • Short, easy to say and spell
  • Fits the product, without describing it
  • Extends to future products
  • Protectable: registries, trademarks
  • Clean connotations in context
  • Gives the logo something to draw

Lessons from renames

Burbn became Instagram once the product changed. dotCloud became Docker after its project outgrew the company name. A name tied to one feature ("Skills") costs a rename later. Doing it now, before MCP ships, is the cheap moment.

Dev-tool norms

The names that last use a metaphor for the job: Kubernetes (steers containers), Terraform (reshapes the ground), Homebrew (makes your own). Single dictionary words are mostly taken. Loadout, Quiver, Pegboard and Concord all already belong to agent-skill or MCP tools.

Why Shadowboard dropped. "Shadow MCP servers" is now an OWASP risk category (MCP09:2025, unsanctioned MCP servers), and vendors call ungoverned agents and skills "shadow AI". A trust tool for MCP servers shouldn't share a name with the threat it guards against.

Jackfield's checks: free on PyPI and npm, with no agent, skill or MCP project using it. "Jack" also suggests versatility (jack of all trades). Jackfield is also a village in Shropshire, known for its tile museum, which is harmless. Not yet done: a USPTO/EUIPO trademark search and a domain check.

Part 2 · Logo

What the logos had to do

Appropriate, distinctive, simple

Chermayeff & Geismar's test (Sagi Haviv): someone who sees it once or twice can describe it or doodle it. Every concept here is one idea in one sentence.

Avoid the category look

Out for AI dev tools in 2024–26: sparkles, purple-green gradients, node networks, hexagons (the Agent Skills mark is already one), terminal prompts, robots, brains. Ink plus one blue, no gradients.

16 px to sticker

You need a favicon, app header, README, desktop icon and stickers. Every concept was rendered at 16, 32 and 64 px, in one colour, reversed, blurred, mirrored and rotated, and placed next to real marks from Vercel, Framer, Dropbox, Tidal, GitHub, Docker, Patreon and Stripe.

Five directions for Jackfield

Three evolve the current switchboard mark and two are new, as you asked. Each card shows light and dark tiles, the lockup, and the mark at 64, 32 and 16 px.

A Patched panel

Recommended

Abstract · evolution

A jackfield faceplate with nine sockets. One jack is patched in, and that's the one you look at.

  • Says the name (jacks in a field) and the product (one place to see every agent, with the odd one flagged) in a single shape.

Recommendation and next step

You chose B, Field J, and I recommend the B7, Quiet soft J variant (above). For reference, the original five directions are kept here.

Once you pick a direction, the full logo kit covers:

  1. Final geometry with optical corrections, a small-size cut and a thinner reversed version.
  2. A custom-drawn "jackfield" wordmark (these lockups use outlined IBM Plex), plus horizontal and stacked lockups.
  3. Favicon, app icon and web-icon set; light/dark SVGs wired into the web UI and README.
  4. A one-page usage guide: clear space, minimum size, colours, misuse.

The rename itself (CLI command, package name, docs) is a separate decision, and nothing in the code has changed yet.
